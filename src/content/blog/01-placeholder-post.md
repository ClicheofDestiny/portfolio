I'm not great at focusing and never have been. My attention is short and my thoughts are only strands that constantly slip away. No task gets done unless I write it down first, no matter how minute. Review that security update. Reply to Chad. Grab another coffee.

I think this makes me benefit from pairing more than most. Another person acts as an outlet for my stream of consciousness. But while rubber ducking to someone gives you an outlet, if the other person hasn't spent just as much time reasoning about the problem as you, they can feel a bit like Major Toht...

![Indiana Jones face melting gif](/public/face-melting-indiana-jones.gif)

So I prefer two people rubber ducking to each other with the same context. 


Here is the dynamic I like to see.

The two decide on some small task or milestone. This can be as small as reading a blog post or as large as vibe coding a prototype. They do not divide and conquer, they both attempt to understand the same context or perform the same task. They make progress, get stuck, take notes, amass questions. Then after an allocated amount of time, they come together again.wd


I'm a fan of pairing, and I think my favourite is pairing two people who are of a similar level. When you pair a senior and junior together, the junior learns the senior's way of doing things.
